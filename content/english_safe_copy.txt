Emotion Naming Map
36 Emotion Cards · Full English Safe Copy
Based on publicly available concepts from Pete Walker & Bessel van der Kolk · Fully rephrased

Safety principles applied: second-person language, substantially lowered similarity to source texts, "Inner Critic" used instead of unique original terms, clear attribution.
01. Toxic Shame
Description  What you feel is not ordinary guilt for having done something wrong. It is a bone-deep sense that you yourself are wrong, bad, and undeserving of love.
Mechanism  When an emotional flashback is triggered, toxic shame often becomes the strongest and most central feeling. It frequently originates from early experiences of contempt, neglect, or verbal injury.
Healing Direction  Quiet the inner critic and practice self-compassion. Interrupt self-attack with healthy boundaries, and respond to the wounded part with your adult self.
Safe Practice  When the self-attacking voice appears, gently label it: "This is the inner critic." Place a hand on your chest and say: "I am willing to begin treating myself differently."
02. Fear in Emotional Flashback
Description  A single sentence or glance suddenly pulls you back into that small, frightened feeling. Your body reacts before your mind does—you want to flee or shrink.
Mechanism  When old fear is triggered by the present situation, the nervous system rapidly enters high arousal, producing intense unease or even panic.
Healing Direction  First recognize "this may be an old wound being triggered," then use grounding to bring the body back to the present and build a sense that "I am safe now."
Safe Practice  Press both feet firmly into the ground. Slowly exhale while counting to four. Repeat several times, then quietly say: "I am safe now. This is a memory."
03. Despair in Emotional Flashback
Description  It feels like falling into a deep well—nothing you do will matter, nothing will get better. Your body wants to hide or becomes heavy and numb.
Mechanism  When despair becomes the main triggered feeling, people often enter a state of deep powerlessness and the urge to withdraw.
Healing Direction  Recognize that this is old helplessness being activated, not the whole of present reality. Use the smallest possible action to restore a little sense of "I can still choose."
Safe Practice  Write down "I am still here," then do one tiny completable thing (drink water, open a window, stand and take two steps).
04. Helplessness & Powerlessness
Description  You feel you can do nothing, as if pinned in place. You know what should be done yet cannot move.
Mechanism  During intense emotional activation, people often re-experience the smallness and powerlessness of childhood. Trauma also weakens the sense that "I can influence outcomes."
Healing Direction  Rebuild agency through tiny choices that are fully under your control.
Safe Practice  Move a finger or a toe. Feel "I can still control this small part of my body," then do one small thing decided entirely by you.
05. Small, Young & Vulnerable
Description  Suddenly you feel very small and young, like a child who needs protection yet has no one to protect them.
Mechanism  When old wounds are triggered, body and mind easily regress to the state of early threat.
Healing Direction  Respond to that young part with your adult self, letting it know "I am here now."
Safe Practice  Gently place a hand on your heart or arm and say to the young part inside: "I see you. I am here."
06. Abandonment Depression
Description  When things grow quiet, emptiness and pain rise up—like reaching the dead end of a blind alley, helpless and hopeless.
Mechanism  This deep emptiness and pain often relate to early emotional neglect or abandonment, and surface more easily when alone.
Healing Direction  Keep company with the emptiness instead of rushing to fill or escape it. Allow the process of mourning to unfold slowly.
Safe Practice  Place a hand on your chest and say: "I am willing to stay with this emptiness rather than immediately flee or fill it." Allow yourself a few quiet moments.
07. Complex Abandonment
Description  Shame, fear and melancholy tangle together, making you both want closeness and want to run. The inside feels chaotic.
Mechanism  When multiple old emotions are triggered at once, people often fall into the contradiction of longing for connection while fearing injury.
Healing Direction  Name the feelings first, then look at them separately instead of being overwhelmed by the whole mass.
Safe Practice  On paper write "shame," "fear," and "melancholy" separately, then tell yourself: "They can exist at the same time. For now I am only looking."
08. Self-Hatred / Self-Loathing
Description  It is not merely dislike of yourself. It is a visceral aversion and the urge to attack yourself.
Mechanism  When the inner critic becomes extreme, people develop intense aversion and aggression toward themselves.
Healing Direction  Identify and label the inner critic, and redirect the aggression away from yourself.
Safe Practice  When self-attack appears, clearly say: "This is the inner critic." Then place a hand on your chest and remind yourself: "I deserve to be treated more gently."
09. Self-Abandonment
Description  You always put others first. You may not even notice when you are hungry, tired or in pain—essentially abandoning yourself.
Mechanism  If you were long neglected early on, it is easy to learn to satisfy others first and lose awareness of your own needs.
Healing Direction  Re-learn to bring "yourself" back into view, starting with small bodily signals.
Safe Practice  Ask yourself three questions: "Am I hungry? Tired? Do I need rest?" Then respond to at least one of them.
10. Inner Critic Attack
Description  That voice in your head that constantly finds fault and says you are not good enough is often the internalization of early criticism.
Mechanism  The inner critic usually comes from the attitudes of important early figures. It tries to "protect" you by attacking you, at the cost of ongoing self-depletion.
Healing Direction  Notice its appearance with mindfulness, then interrupt it with gentle firmness.
Safe Practice  When it appears, first name it "This is the inner critic," then interrupt with a short sentence: "I do not need to treat myself this way."
11. Social Anxiety
Description  In front of others you constantly feel scrutinized, fear saying the wrong thing, fear being seen through as "not good enough."
Mechanism  When shame is strong, attention in social settings easily focuses excessively on "how others see me."
Healing Direction  Lower the catastrophic expectation of being "found out," while building new experience through small doses of authentic exposure.
Safe Practice  Prepare one simple opening line or exit phrase in advance to reduce the fear of the unknown.
12. Shame-Based Anger
Description  Anger that rises quickly when you feel exposed, criticized or small—often a secondary reaction to underlying shame.
Mechanism  When toxic shame is activated, anger can surge as a way to regain a sense of power or to push the threat away.
Healing Direction  Look beneath the anger for the shame or fear it is protecting, and respond to that deeper layer.
Safe Practice  When anger flares, pause and ask: "What just made me feel small or exposed?" Name that softer feeling first.
13. Anger & Aggression
Description  A surge of heat, the urge to push back, control, or strike—whether expressed outward or turned inward.
Mechanism  Anger is a boundary-protecting emotion. In trauma it may become either chronically suppressed or easily explosive.
Healing Direction  Allow anger to exist as information about boundaries, and find safe ways to express or channel it.
Safe Practice  In a private, safe space (journal, shower, alone), allow yourself to speak the angry words without having to justify them.
14. Freeze Response
Description  You know you should act, flee or speak, yet the body feels pinned and unable to move. Energy is stuck.
Mechanism  Freeze is a survival response. Uncompleted fight-or-flight energy can remain held in the body.
Healing Direction  Respect the freeze. Do not force movement. Gently mobilize only when the body shows readiness.
Safe Practice  Allow yourself to stay still at first and simply breathe. When the body feels even a little ready, make the smallest possible movement.
15. Overwhelm / Collapse
Description  You feel you are about to fall apart. Emotion, pressure and fatigue surge together; you want to cry or drop.
Mechanism  When the nervous system exceeds its window of tolerance, collapse or overwhelm can appear as a protective shutdown.
Healing Direction  Reduce input, restore basic safety and regulation, then return to one small manageable piece.
Safe Practice  Stop adding new tasks. Sit or lie down. Name three things you can see right now. Breathe out longer than you breathe in.
16. Numbing & Dissociation
Description  You are not "over it"—you feel switched off. Joy and sadness both become muted, as if behind a layer of cotton.
Mechanism  To survive unbearable pain, the nervous system may turn down the volume on feeling.
Healing Direction  Gently reconnect with bodily sensation without forcing yourself to "feel more."
Safe Practice  Notice one small physical sensation (temperature of the hands, the rise and fall of the breath). Simply notice, without judging or demanding change.
17. Fawning / People-Pleasing
Description  You keep others happy, give constantly, and lose track of yourself—"If I am good enough, you will not leave."
Mechanism  When love and safety once depended on pleasing others, fawning can become an automatic survival strategy.
Healing Direction  Practice keeping a little space for yourself in small matters, and experience that relationships do not instantly collapse when you are imperfect.
Safe Practice  Today, respond to one small request with "I need to think about it first," then notice what happens in your body.
18. Flight Response
Description  The strong urge to escape—leave the room, leave the conversation, leave the feeling—before anything is resolved.
Mechanism  Flight is a survival response. When old danger is sensed, the system prioritizes getting away.
Healing Direction  Acknowledge the urge to flee, then experiment with staying for one more breath or one more minute when it is actually safe.
Safe Practice  When the urge to leave arises, pause and name it: "This is the flight response." Then decide whether leaving is necessary or whether you can stay a little longer.
19. Fight Response
Description  The body braces to confront, argue, control or overpower. Softness feels dangerous.
Mechanism  Fight is a protective mobilization. In trauma it can become a default way of creating safety through dominance or control.
Healing Direction  Redirect the protective energy from controlling others toward protecting your own real boundaries.
Safe Practice  When the fighting energy rises, ask: "What am I actually trying to protect right now—my boundary or my sense of control?"
20. Hypervigilance
Description  You are always scanning for danger, reading faces, waiting for the other shoe to drop. Rest feels unsafe.
Mechanism  After prolonged threat, the nervous system can remain on high alert even when the present is relatively safe.
Healing Direction  Gradually teach the body that periods of safety can exist, using repeated experiences of "nothing bad happened."
Safe Practice  Once a day, deliberately look around and name five things that indicate you are safe in this moment.
21. Body Memory Trigger
Description  A smell, posture, tone of voice or sensation suddenly floods the body with old feeling before the mind understands why.
Mechanism  Trauma is stored not only in narrative memory but in the body. Sensory cues can reactivate those implicit memories.
Healing Direction  Orient to the present with the senses, and remind the body that the danger is not happening now.
Safe Practice  Use 5-4-3-2-1: name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.
22. Emptiness & Hollowness
Description  There is a hollow place inside that nothing seems to fill. Everything feels muted, as if living on mute.
Mechanism  Emptiness often relates to emotional numbing, early neglect, or long-term suppression of feeling.
Healing Direction  Keep company with the emptiness rather than rushing to fill it with activity, consumption or relationships.
Safe Practice  Do not hurry to fill it. Simply sit with the emptiness for one minute and notice whether anything shifts.
23. Helplessness
Description  The sense that "nothing I do will matter." The feeling of being able to choose or act has gone missing.
Mechanism  Repeated experiences of powerlessness can erode the belief that one can influence outcomes.
Healing Direction  Rebuild the sense of "I can affect things" through tiny choices that are fully under your control.
Safe Practice  Do one small thing you can completely control (what to wear, what to drink, which task to do first).
24. Powerlessness
Description  You feel stripped of influence. Decisions, outcomes and even your own reactions seem outside your hands.
Mechanism  Chronic powerlessness in early environments can leave a lasting imprint on the sense of agency.
Healing Direction  Reclaim micro-agency: start with the smallest domain where choice is still available.
Safe Practice  Choose one tiny preference today and honor it without explaining or justifying it to anyone.
25. Self-Doubt
Description  You constantly feel you have not done well enough, chosen wrongly, or are not smart enough. Decisions loop endlessly.
Mechanism  When self-worth is fragile, the mind keeps second-guessing to avoid the pain of being "wrong."
Healing Direction  Practice making "good enough" decisions and tolerating the residual uncertainty.
Safe Practice  Set a short time limit for a small decision, choose, and then deliberately stop reviewing it for the rest of the day.
26. Perfectionism Pressure
Description  You push yourself with relentless standards. Rest feels like failure; "good enough" feels dangerous.
Mechanism  Perfectionism often serves as a strategy to avoid shame or criticism by staying ahead of judgment.
Healing Direction  Experiment with deliberate imperfection in low-stakes areas and observe that the feared catastrophe does not occur.
Safe Practice  Intentionally leave one small task "good enough" rather than perfect, and notice what the inner critic says.
27. Emotional Numbing
Description  Feelings are distant. You know you "should" feel something, yet the volume is turned very low.
Mechanism  Numbing is a protective adaptation when feeling was once too costly or unsafe.
Healing Direction  Invite sensation back in small, tolerable doses without demanding full emotional intensity.
Safe Practice  Once a day, ask: "What am I sensing in my body right now?" Stay with the answer for three breaths.
28. Fear
Description  A tight, source-less tension that makes you want to flee or shrink. The body reacts before the mind understands.
Mechanism  Fear is a life-protecting signal, yet when old wounds are triggered it may be out of proportion to present reality.
Healing Direction  Build a sense of "secure base" so the body knows there is somewhere safe to return to.
Safe Practice  Identify one safe object or place and tell yourself: "I can return here." Actually feel its presence.
29. Suppressed Anger
Description  The fire held at the bottom—afraid to express, quick to self-blame the moment it rises.
Mechanism  When anger is chronically suppressed it may turn against the self or erupt unpredictably. Healthy anger is a guardian of boundaries.
Healing Direction  Allow anger to exist and express it in safe ways instead of continuing to suppress or convert it into self-blame.
Safe Practice  In a safe private setting, allow yourself to speak the angry words without needing to make them reasonable.
30. Grief / Sadness
Description  A heavy ache in the chest. You want to cry but cannot, or once you start you cannot stop.
Mechanism  Grief is a natural channel for releasing loss and pain. Suppressing it keeps the pain stuck in other forms.
Healing Direction  Allow tears and sadness to move rather than suppressing them or rushing to "cheer up."
Safe Practice  If tears will not come, simply allow the feeling of "wanting to cry" to be present—neither forcing nor immediately stopping it.
31. Guilt
Description  You quickly assume it is your fault, rush to apologize, and when someone is unhappy your first thought is "Did I cause this?"
Mechanism  If you were early on made responsible for others' emotions, it is easy to take on excessive responsibility.
Healing Direction  Distinguish what truly belongs to you from what has been over-assumed.
Safe Practice  Ask: "If someone else were in the same situation, would I blame them the same way?" Use that standard to reassess.
32. Everyday Shame
Description  A lighter, everyday sense of not being good enough, of being about to be found wanting—less bone-deep than toxic shame, yet still draining.
Mechanism  Everyday shame is a common background tone when self-worth is fragile.
Healing Direction  Practice being seen in your imperfection and accumulate new corrective experiences.
Safe Practice  Share one small imperfection with a trusted person and practice being seen without being abandoned.
33. Despair
Description  The deep-well feeling that "nothing I do will help; it will never get better."
Mechanism  When hope has been repeatedly defeated or old helplessness is triggered, despair can become the dominant feeling.
Healing Direction  Shrink the time scale to "today" and counter global hopelessness with the smallest possible action.
Safe Practice  Do only the smallest step available today. Do not look at the future. Do not demand that you feel hopeful.
34. Feeling Wronged / Unseen Hurt
Description  You are hurting yet cannot speak it. You hold it in, convinced that speaking would change nothing.
Mechanism  When important feelings went long unanswered, it is easy to learn to swallow the sense of being wronged.
Healing Direction  First give your own feelings the recognition they need, then decide whether to share them with others.
Safe Practice  In a journal write: "Today I felt wronged because..." Write it only for yourself—complete one act of self-recognition.
35. Unworthiness
Description  When something good arrives, the first reaction is "I don't deserve this" or "They will eventually see I am not that good."
Mechanism  When self-worth is built on "I must be good enough to be accepted," it becomes hard to receive what rightfully belongs to you.
Healing Direction  Practice receiving kindness without explanation or refusal, challenging the automatic "I don't deserve it."
Safe Practice  Accept one small kindness or compliment. Say only "thank you"—no explaining, no refusing, no diminishing yourself.
36. Fragile Self-Worth
Description  Self-worth feels like thin ice. The slightest breeze cracks it. It needs constant external approval to stay intact.
Mechanism  When self-worth depends mainly on outside evaluation, it becomes unstable and easily shattered.
Healing Direction  Through steady small self-affirmations, gradually thicken the inner sense of worth.
Safe Practice  Each day write down three things you did, however small, as an internal record.
Attribution & Use
This tool reworks publicly available concepts from Pete Walker's Complex PTSD: From Surviving to Thriving and Bessel van der Kolk's The Body Keeps the Score into original second-person language for self-exploration and emotion naming. It is not an official product of either author.
It does not constitute medical or psychological advice. If distress is severe or persistent, please seek qualified professional support.
